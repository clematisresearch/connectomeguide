# Written Submission Guide: Track 1 (Synapse-Level Connectomics)

This guide provides instructions on formatting, structuring, and submitting your written report for **Track 1: Synapse-Level Connectomics (Flies)**.

## 2. Word Count & Document Structure by Level

The required report structure depends on the track level you are completing:

### A. Introductory Level (Research Commentary)
* **Target Word Count:** approximately **500–1,000 words** (excluding figures and references).
* **Format:** Concise research commentary focusing on a selected neuron / microcircuit.
* **Suggested Sections:**
  1. **Neuron Overview & Motivation (50–150 words):** Identification of the neuron (name, type, Body ID) and general biological motivation.
  2. **Morphology & Anatomical Projections (150–300 words):** 3D shape, brain regions where it branches, dendrites vs axon terminals, and labeled 3D figure from Neuroglancer.
  3. **Synaptic Connectivity & Circuit Partners (200–350 words):** Quantitative table/summary of top upstream and downstream synaptic partners, connection weights, and percentages.
  4. **Functional Interpretation & Literature Context (100–250 words):** Connecting connectivity to fly behavior, predicted neurotransmitters, and citations from scientific literature.
  5. **Declaration of Generative AI and AI-Assisted Technologies in the Writing Process:** Required disclosure heading.
  6. **References:** Citations for dataset ([Berg et al., 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.09.680999v2)), software (Neuroglancer, neuPrint), and academic papers.

### B. Advanced Level (Research Manuscript)
* **Target Word Count:** approximately **2,000–4,000 words** (excluding references).
* **Format:** Scientific research manuscript reporting original computational analysis of the connectome.
* **Suggested Sections:**
  1. **Abstract (150–250 words)**
  2. **Introduction (200–800 words)**
  3. **Materials and Methods (600–1,200 words)** (Dataset details, filtering criteria, Python API pipeline, network/statistical analyses, software packages)
  4. **Results (400–800 words)** (Figures, tables, connectivity heatmaps, graph theoretical metrics)
  5. **Discussion (400–800 words)** (Biological interpretation, circuit mechanisms, limitations, future directions)
  6. **Acknowledgements**
  7. **Declaration of Generative AI and AI-Assisted Technologies in the Writing Process**
  8. **References**

## 3. Formatting Guidelines

While there is no strict template, following standard scholarly presentation ensures that your work is clear and easy for judges to evaluate:

* **File Format:** PDF format is required.
* **Typography:** Clean, readable font (e.g., Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman, 11–12 pt).
* **Line Spacing:** 1.15 to 1.5 line spacing.
* **Margins:** Standard 1-inch (2.54 cm) margins.
* **Figures & Tables:** Number all figures (e.g., *Figure 1: 3D reconstruction of LC10a*) and tables, and include informative captions describing what is shown.
* **Citations:** Consistent citation style (APA, Harvard, Vancouver, or Nature style).

## 4. Pre-Submission Checklist

Before submitting your written report through the Google Form, verify the following:

- [ ] My report clearly states the **competition track (Track 1)** and **level attempted (Introductory or Advanced)**.
- [ ] My report includes the **Body ID(s)** and **cell type names** of all analyzed neurons.
- [ ] All figures have clear captions, labeled axes/regions, and appropriate color scales.
- [ ] Quantitative values (synapse counts, connection percentages) are reported accurately.
- [ ] The **Declaration of Generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the writing process** section heading is included.
- [ ] All external sources, datasets, and software are properly cited in the References section.
- [ ] The document is saved as a PDF and ready for upload.
