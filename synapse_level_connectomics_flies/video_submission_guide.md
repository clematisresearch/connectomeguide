# Video Submission Guide: Track 1 (Synapse-Level Connectomics)

The **video submission** is an essential deliverable for Connectome 2026–2027. Unlike the written report (which is written for a neuroscience audience), your video is intended for a **general audience**—including fellow students, educators, and the broader scientific community.

This is your opportunity to share your research journey, show off beautiful 3D neuron visualizations, explain how the circuit works, and communicate your findings in an engaging and accessible way.

---

## 1. Video Requirements & Rules

* **Duration:** Maximum **10 minutes** (concise videos between 3–7 minutes are often ideal).
* **Audience:** General audience (explain concepts clearly without excessive jargon; define terms when used).
* **Face Camera:** You are **not required to show your face**, though you are welcome to do so if you wish.
* **Voiceover:** Narration must be spoken in **your own voice**. Generative AI synthetic voices or automated text-to-speech tools are **strictly prohibited**.
* **Visuals:** We strongly encourage including screen recordings of **Neuroglancer** or **neuPrint** to bring your neurons and circuits to life in 3D!

---

## 2. Suggested Video Formats

You have full creative freedom in how you present your work. Effective formats include:

1. **Interactive Screen Walkthrough:**  
   Record your screen as you navigate Neuroglancer, rotating your neuron in 3D, showing where its branches project across brain regions, and demonstrating how it connects to upstream and downstream partners.
2. **Slide Presentation with Narration:**  
   Present a clear slide deck (e.g., PowerPoint, Canva, Google Slides, Keynote) covering your motivation, research question, key circuit figures, and conclusions.
3. **Hybrid Presentation:**  
   Combine introductory slides with live Neuroglancer screen captures and connectivity charts.
4. **Short-Form or Animated Video:**  
   A dynamic, creative overview explaining the biological problem, how the connectome helps solve it, and what you discovered.

---

## 3. Recommended Video Structure

A strong, engaging 4–7 minute video might follow this flow:

1. **The Hook & Motivation (30–60 seconds):**  
   Introduce the behavioral question: Why is this neuron or circuit interesting? (e.g., *"How does a fly avoid a fly swatter in a split second? Today, we're tracing the visual circuits behind this escape reflex."*)
2. **Meet the Neuron in 3D (1–2 minutes):**  
   Show the neuron in Neuroglancer. Explain its shape, which brain regions (neuropils) it connects to, and whether it receives visual, olfactory, or motor signals.
3. **The Synaptic Circuit (1–2 minutes):**  
   Highlight the top upstream inputs and downstream outputs. Explain what kinds of signals flow into the cell and where it sends its outputs.
4. **Biological Meaning & Conclusion (1 minute):**  
   Summarize what this wiring tells us about fly behavior or neural computation. Reflect briefly on what you learned during the project.

---

## 4. Free Recording Tools

You do not need professional recording equipment. Free, simple tools include:

* **OBS Studio (Open Broadcaster Software):** Free, open-source screen recording for Windows, Mac, and Linux ([obsproject.com](https://obsproject.com/)).
* **Zoom / Microsoft Teams:** Start a solo meeting, share your screen, and record locally.
* **QuickTime Player (Mac):** Built-in screen recording (`File → New Screen Recording`).
* **Canva / Loom:** Record yourself presenting slides directly in the browser.

---

## 5. How to Submit Your Video

All video submissions must be shared on the **Clematis Discord**:

1. Join the Discord community: [https://tinyurl.com/discordclematis](https://discord.com/invite/MFFbJQFtvW)
2. Navigate to the **#Connectome2026-2027** channel.
3. Post your video:
   * You may upload the video file directly (MP4 / WebM), or
   * Share an accessible link (YouTube, Google Drive, Vimeo, OneDrive, Dropbox, or LinkedIn).
4. Include a brief caption in your post containing:
   * **Project Title**
   * **Your Name**
   * **Track & Level** (e.g., *Track 1: Synapse-Level Connectomics – Introductory Level*)
   * **Neuron / Cell Type Analyzed** (e.g., `LC10a`, Body ID: `10001`)
   * **A short 2–3 sentence summary** of what your video explores.

We look forward to seeing your 3D visualizations and hearing about your research journey! 🪰
