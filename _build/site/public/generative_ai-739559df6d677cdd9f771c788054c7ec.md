# GenAI Policy

Connectome2026 recognizes that generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) tools are becoming increasingly common in education, research, and industry. Participants are welcome to use generative AI tools to support their learning and research process, provided that such use is disclosed appropriately and does not replace the participant's own scientific thinking and analysis.

Participants remain fully responsible for the accuracy, integrity, and originality of all submitted work.

---

## Permitted Uses

Examples of acceptable uses include:

- Learning neuroscience, programming, statistics, or related concepts
- Brainstorming research questions or project ideas
- Explaining scientific papers or technical concepts
- Debugging code
- Improving grammar, clarity, or writing style
- Generating example code snippets
- Assisting with data visualization
- Receiving feedback on report structure or presentation design

---

## Responsibilities of Participants

Participants must:

- Critically evaluate any information generated by AI tools
- Verify scientific claims, references, and code before using them
- Ensure that all conclusions and interpretations are supported by evidence
- Remain the primary author and decision-maker throughout the research process

Participants are responsible for any errors, inaccuracies, fabricated references, or misleading statements that appear in their submissions, regardless of whether they originated from a generative AI tool.

---

## Disclosure Requirement

If generative AI tools were used in preparing a submission, participants must include a brief **Generative AI Use Disclosure** at the end of their report.

The disclosure should describe:

- Which tool(s) were used
- How they were used
- The extent of their contribution

### Example 1

```{admonition} Generative AI Use Disclosure
ChatGPT (OpenAI) was used to help explain neuroscience concepts, debug Python code, and improve the clarity and grammar of portions of the written report. All analyses, interpretations, conclusions, and final writing decisions were made by the participant.
````

### Example 2

```{admonition} Generative AI Use Disclosure
Claude (Anthropic) was used to assist with brainstorming potential research questions and suggesting approaches for data visualization. The participant independently conducted all analyses and prepared all final figures and conclusions.
```

---

## Prohibited Uses

Participants may not:

* Present AI-generated content as scientific evidence without verification
* Fabricate results, analyses, figures, references, or citations
* Submit work that they do not understand
* Use generative AI to impersonate another individual
* Use AI-generated voices in video submissions

For video submissions containing narration or spoken commentary, the voice must be the participant's own voice.

---

## Our Philosophy

Connectome2026 views generative AI as a tool that can support learning, creativity, and research when used responsibly.

We are not interested in whether participants used AI. We are interested in whether participants can think critically, engage meaningfully with the data, interpret evidence thoughtfully, and communicate their work clearly. Transparency and responsible use are therefore more important than avoiding AI altogether.
