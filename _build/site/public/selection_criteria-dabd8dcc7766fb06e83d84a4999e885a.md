# Overall Principles

Submissions will be evaluated based on the quality of the participant's engagement with the competition, the strength of their scientific reasoning, the clarity of their communication, and the extent to which they make effective use of the provided datasets and tools.

Because Connectome2026 is a learning-focused research competition, judges will value:

* Genuine engagement with the data and tools
* Clear scientific thinking and reasoning
* Appropriate use of evidence, figures, and citations
* Originality, curiosity, and initiative
* Effective communication to the intended audience
* Level-appropriate ambition and depth

Participants will not be judged solely on whether their results are "correct." Research often involves exploratory analysis, unexpected findings, and revisions of thinking. What matters most is whether the submission demonstrates meaningful scientific inquiry and clear communication.

# Written Report Selection Criteria

The written report is intended to resemble a scientific research report or manuscript, with expectations varying according to the competition level attempted.

## 1. Scientific Question and Rationale

**How clearly does the report define the research question, motivation, or line of inquiry?**

### Excellent (4)

The report presents a clear, focused, and well-motivated research question or investigative aim. The rationale is compelling and grounded in neuroscience or connectomics.

### Good (3)

The report identifies a clear question or aim and provides a reasonable explanation for why it matters.

### Satisfactory (2)

The report indicates an overall topic or direction, but the rationale is somewhat general or underdeveloped.

### Limited (1)

The report lacks a clear question, purpose, or rationale.

---

## 2. Engagement with Data, Tools, and Methods

**How effectively does the participant use the provided datasets, connectomics tools, and analytical methods?**

### Excellent (4)

The report shows strong and appropriate use of the datasets and tools. Methods are used thoughtfully and in a way that supports the investigation.

### Good (3)

The report demonstrates clear engagement with the datasets and tools, with only minor gaps in explanation or use.

### Satisfactory (2)

The report shows some use of the provided resources, but the engagement is limited, uneven, or mostly descriptive.

### Limited (1)

The report shows little meaningful engagement with the datasets, tools, or analysis process.

---

## 3. Analysis, Interpretation, and Scientific Insight

**How well does the participant analyze the data and interpret the findings?**

### Excellent (4)

The analysis is well executed and the interpretation is insightful, balanced, and evidence-based. The report demonstrates strong understanding of connectomics and neuroscience concepts.

### Good (3)

The analysis is appropriate and the interpretation is generally sound, with some evidence of deeper scientific understanding.

### Satisfactory (2)

The analysis is basic or incomplete, and the interpretation is mostly descriptive with limited insight.

### Limited (1)

The analysis is minimal, unclear, or not meaningfully interpreted.

---

## 4. Organization, Clarity, and Writing Quality

**How clearly is the report written and structured?**

### Excellent (4)

The report is well organized, coherent, and easy to follow. Writing is clear and polished, and the structure supports the scientific content effectively.

### Good (3)

The report is well structured and mostly clear, with only minor issues in flow or expression.

### Satisfactory (2)

The report is understandable but may have issues with organization, clarity, or consistency.

### Limited (1)

The report is difficult to follow or poorly organized.

---

## 5. Figures, Citations, and Scholarly Presentation

**How effectively does the participant use figures, references, and scientific presentation conventions?**

### Excellent (4)

Figures are used effectively and enhance understanding. Citations are appropriate and consistent. The report reflects careful scholarly presentation.

### Good (3)

Figures and citations are used appropriately, with only minor issues in style or formatting.

### Satisfactory (2)

Some figures or references are included, but they are limited in usefulness, incomplete, or inconsistently presented.

### Limited (1)

Figures and citations are missing, minimal, or used poorly.

# Video Submission Selection Criteria

The video submission is intended for a broader audience, including peers, educators, family members, and the wider Clematis community. It should communicate the participant's project, ideas, or research journey in an engaging and accessible way.

## 1. Communication to a General Audience

**How effectively does the video explain the project to viewers who may not have a neuroscience background?**

### Excellent (4)

The video is highly accessible, clear, and engaging for a broad audience. Complex ideas are explained effectively without oversimplification.

### Good (3)

The video is understandable to a general audience, with only minor areas that may benefit from clearer explanation.

### Satisfactory (2)

The video communicates the project, but some parts may be too technical, vague, or difficult for a non-specialist audience.

### Limited (1)

The video is difficult to follow or is not meaningfully adapted for a general audience.

---

## 2. Storytelling, Structure, and Flow

**How well is the video organized and presented?**

### Excellent (4)

The video has a strong narrative structure and a clear flow, making it engaging and easy to follow from beginning to end.

### Good (3)

The video is well organized and mostly easy to follow.

### Satisfactory (2)

The video has some structure, but the flow is uneven or difficult to follow in places.

### Limited (1)

The video lacks structure or is difficult to understand.

---

## 3. Engagement with the Project

**Does the video demonstrate meaningful engagement with the competition, dataset, or research process?**

### Excellent (4)

The video clearly reflects meaningful engagement with the project, including thoughtful use of tools, datasets, analyses, or research ideas.

### Good (3)

The video shows clear engagement and some reflection on the project or learning process.

### Satisfactory (2)

The video shows basic engagement, but the connection to the project is limited or superficial.

### Limited (1)

The video shows little meaningful engagement with the project.

---

## 4. Visual and Creative Communication

**How effectively does the participant use visuals, slides, animation, screen recordings, or other creative elements?**

### Excellent (4)

Visuals are polished, effective, and used purposefully to strengthen communication and understanding.

### Good (3)

Visuals are clear and helpful, with only minor room for improvement.

### Satisfactory (2)

Visuals are present but not consistently effective or well integrated into the presentation.

### Limited (1)

Visuals are minimal, unclear, or distracting.

---

## 5. Authenticity and Presentation Quality

**How effectively does the participant communicate their own experience, perspective, or scientific journey?**

### Excellent (4)

The video feels authentic, confident, and well prepared. The participant's voice and perspective come through clearly.

### Good (3)

The video is clear and well presented, with a credible personal or project voice.

### Satisfactory (2)

The video is understandable, but the delivery is uneven or somewhat generic.

### Limited (1)

The video is poorly presented or lacks a clear personal or project voice.

---

# How Competition Levels Will Be Considered

Submissions will be evaluated relative to the level attempted.

* **Introductory Level submissions** are expected to demonstrate guided engagement, foundational understanding, and clear communication.
* **Advanced Level submissions** are expected to show greater independence, deeper analysis, and more original inquiry.

Participants are not expected to perform advanced analyses in order to produce a strong Introductory-Level submission.

---

# Participation Certificate versus Awards

For participation certificate eligibility, the emphasis is on meaningful effort and engagement with the competition.

For awards and prizes, judges will look for stronger performance across the selection criteria, with particular attention to scientific reasoning, originality, depth of analysis, and communication quality.
